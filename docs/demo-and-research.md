# Demo, participant research, and emotional impact

## Direction

The team considers the video crucial and wants the emotional impact of these references:

- Standard Response: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eNsAGHFnFJY
- homecookd: https://devpost.com/software/homecookd
- homecookd embedded video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8Ibl2QnL9dw

Both video transcripts and selected visuals were reviewed. Standard Response introduces Anthony and Justin, explains an emergency-response failure, includes firefighter knowledge, demonstrates a call, and returns to the people at the end. Its outcome figures are presented as simulations. homecookd introduces Shreya and her grandmother, establishes their separation, then shows real cooking and the grandmother's voice within the product experience.

For this product, build toward someone handing off an administrative task they have struggled to manage. Use the person's actual experience and reaction. Do not copy another project's tragedy or imply your prototype produced an unverified outcome.

## Finding a participant

The team has not yet found someone and is able to drive around SF. Driving helps after a willing participant is identified; it does not create trust. Start with attendees, mentors, parents, relatives, friends, and warm introductions. A caregiver who handled family bills is a valid participant. Disclose relationships when relevant.

Allow 20–30 minutes to seek an introduction, then send two teammates to the confirmed meeting while the others work. Avoid spending the evening approaching patients outside hospitals. If personal introductions fail, contact an organization the team already knows before visiting. No outreach was sent by the assistant.

Suggested initial ask:

> We're students building a tool to help with medical billing paperwork. We're looking for someone who has dealt with confusing bills or repeated insurance calls for a 10-minute conversation. We don't need your bill, diagnosis, insurance details, or account information. We're interested in what the process was like. We'd ask separately before recording or using anything in our demo.

## Privacy and consent

The first ask is an interview, not access to medical records. A real person's experience can establish the problem while a separate synthetic case demonstrates the product.

Offer participation in stages: private unrecorded conversation; optional anonymous audio; optional on-camera interview; optional prototype trial with a fictional sample. Ask separately at each step. Explain whether the final video is public or judge-only, allow excerpt review, and avoid identifying visuals if anonymity is requested. No diagnosis or exact bill amount is needed for a useful story.

Do not call interview feedback a customer success testimonial. Never edit a comment about hypothetical usefulness into a claim of money saved. Label synthetic data and simulated interactions when shown. Do not clone a participant's voice or fabricate their reaction.

## Interview prompts

- Walk me through what happened when you received the bill.
- What did you do next?
- What happened on the last call?
- What part took the most effort?
- What else were you trying to do that day?
- What would you want someone else to take care of?

After a prototype trial: what is clearer now, which part would have helped, what would you trust it to do next, and what would you still want to approve? Do not script their answers or request a predetermined emotional response.

## Suggested two-minute film

| Time | Beat |
| --- | --- |
| 0:00–0:15 | Strongest genuine interview sentence, with the person or permitted supporting footage |
| 0:15–0:30 | Specific billing problem and unresolved question |
| 0:30–0:45 | Document/sample input and the task the person wants help with |
| 0:45–1:20 | One working sequence: investigate, contact, handle a request, or follow up |
| 1:20–1:40 | Exact documented change, with pending and simulated outcomes labeled |
| 1:40–1:55 | Person's genuine response to what they saw |
| 1:55–2:00 | Product name and a short promise supported by the demo |

Show one agent decision and its consequence. For example, a representative requests an EOB, the agent obtains it, and the case resumes. A voicemail and scheduled follow-up are valid results if that is what happened. No confirmed savings are required to show useful progress.

Use a quiet room, eye-level phone camera, clear audio, captions, and appropriately redacted footage. If nobody participates, use a genuine team experience if available or a clearly labeled synthetic scenario. Never invent a testimonial to meet the deadline.
