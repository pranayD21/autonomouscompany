# Product walkthroughs and participant research

These rules apply to anyone talking with people about their claims experience or showing the product to others.

## Participants

Warm introductions work best. A caregiver who handled family bills is a valid participant. Disclose relationships when relevant. Do not approach patients outside hospitals.

## Privacy and consent

The first ask is an interview, not access to medical records. A real person's experience can establish the problem while a separate synthetic case shows the product.

Offer participation in stages: private unrecorded conversation; optional anonymous audio; optional on-camera interview; optional product trial with a fictional sample. Ask separately at each step. Explain whether any recording is public or private, allow excerpt review, and avoid identifying visuals if anonymity is requested. No diagnosis or exact bill amount is needed for a useful story. Never name an individual customer in this repository.

Do not call interview feedback a customer success testimonial. Never edit a comment about hypothetical usefulness into a claim of money saved. Label synthetic data and simulated interactions when shown. Do not clone a participant's voice or fabricate their reaction.

## Interview prompts

- Walk me through what happened when you received the bill.
- What did you do next?
- What happened on the last call?
- What part took the most effort?
- What else were you trying to do that day?
- What would you want someone else to take care of?

After a product trial: what is clearer now, which part would have helped, what would you trust it to do next, and what would you still want to approve? Do not script their answers or request a predetermined emotional response.

## Showing the product

Use the person's actual experience and reaction. Do not imply Assurance produced an unverified outcome.

Show one agent decision and its consequence. For example, a representative requests an EOB, the agent obtains it, and the case resumes. A voicemail and scheduled follow-up are valid results if that is what happened. No confirmed savings are required to show useful progress. Label pending and simulated outcomes.

Redact footage appropriately. If no participant is available, use a genuine team experience or a clearly labeled synthetic scenario, such as the [upload packet](demo-upload-artifacts.md). Never invent a testimonial.
